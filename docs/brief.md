# Cooking Game — Product Brief for Claude Code

Oct 6, 2026 · @Moe

## Concept

A cartoony, commute-length native iOS game: you cook a dish from a cuisine by choosing ingredients and amounts, and an AI judge scores how well your choices fit that cuisine's logic. Players get better at real cooking, which is the point. This is a SkasieHI offering under the "AI for HI" banner, alongside the audio engineering companion.

The scoring target is not one canonical recipe. It is the cuisine's grammar: characteristic ingredient families and ratios. A player who invents a dish that doesn't exist but nails Sichuan's layered numbing, sour, and fragrance should score well.

Working model: Claude owns the project and the product; Moe advises, consents, shapes, reviews, and has the final word on completion. Claude may disagree hard and decline scope creep.

## Core loop and UX

One round takes 60 to 120 seconds. The player is given a cuisine (e.g. Sichuan, Japanese, Italian) and a dish name, picks a cooking vessel, adds ingredients from a palette with amounts, submits, and gets a score, a one-line judge note, and one educational card.

1. **Vessel** sits in the center of the screen: pot, pan, skillet, wok, baking dish, bread pan. The icon changes per dish. Cartoony, flat, two or three colors per screen.
2. **Ingredient palette** along the bottom or side: 12 to 20 options per round, mixed real and decoy ingredients. Tap to add; a dial or stepper sets the amount in simple units (pinch, tsp, tbsp, cup, 100 g). Amounts matter as ratios, not absolutes.
3. **Sound** on every add: sizzle for fat and aromatics, boil for liquids, a gas-flame click when the vessel changes, a clatter when you dump dry goods. Sound is the dopamine; treat it as a first-class feature, not polish.
4. **Submit** triggers the judge: score out of 100, the one-line verdict, the card.
5. **Session** = 3 to 5 rounds, then a summary. No timers, no lives, no streak shaming.

Sound sourcing: public-domain or licensed libraries for the MVP, with Moe's own recordings through the studio chain replacing them before launch.

Accessibility: all sound cues must have a visual twin (steam puff, bubble animation) so the game works muted on a train.

## Scoring engine

Score against a **cuisine profile**, not a recipe. Each cuisine, and each dish within it, has a hand-authored profile that the engine compares the player's submission against. The AI judge writes the feedback; it does not compute the score. This keeps scoring deterministic, cheap, testable, and offline-capable.

**Profile structure per dish**

| Field | Meaning | Example (mapo tofu, Sichuan) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Required families | Ingredient families that must be present | protein (tofu), fermented bean (doubanjiang), numbing (Sichuan peppercorn), aromatic (garlic, ginger, scallion) |
| Optional families | Present in many versions, not penalized if absent | ground pork, fermented black bean |
| Forbidden / off-cuisine | Signals the player has drifted | cream, cheese, basil, lime |
| Ratio bands | Acceptable range of key ratios, by weight | chili bean paste to tofu 1:8 to 1:12; peppercorn to tofu 1:50 to 1:100 |
| Heat signature | How much heat and numbing, on a 1 to 5 scale | heat 4, numbing 4 |
| Technique tags | Which vessel and method fit | wok, stir-fry, braise |

**Score composition** (out of 100)

- 40 — family coverage: required families present, forbidden ones absent
- 35 — ratio fit: how many key ratios land inside their band, with partial credit for near misses
- 15 — cuisine signature: heat, acid, umami, sweetness within the cuisine's typical envelope
- 10 — vessel and technique match

**Judge feedback** is one sentence, generated by an LLM from the score breakdown plus the profile, in the voice of a cooking-show judge: specific, warm, a little opinionated. Example: "Good aromatics, but that much sugar reads Thai, not Sichuan." Cache feedback per (dish, score pattern) to cut inference cost.

**Decoy design**: every ingredient palette includes ingredients that belong to a neighboring cuisine. Distinguishing Sichuan from Hunan, or Tuscan from Sicilian, is where the learning happens.

## Learning layer

Every round ends with one **educational card**: a short, specific lesson tied to what the player just did. Cards are earned, not browsed. The card for "why dashi pairs kombu with bonito" appears after the player fumbles or nails an umami base, so the lesson lands on a felt moment.

**Card format**: title, two to four sentences, one ratio or rule stated plainly, one line of "try this tonight". Under 60 words. No video, no links in the free tier.

**Tracks** are linear, paid curricula of about 40 lessons per cuisine. Each lesson is a dish plus its card plus a pass threshold; the next lesson unlocks only on a pass. The sequence builds: aromatics base, then fat and heat, then fermented and umami layers, then acid and finish, then full dishes, then regional variation.

**Progression inside a track**

1. Lessons 1 to 8: pantry and aromatics. What the cuisine always starts with.
2. Lessons 9 to 16: ratios. The three or four ratios that define the cuisine.
3. Lessons 17 to 28: core dishes, one technique each.
4. Lessons 29 to 36: regional variants and neighbor-cuisine decoys.
5. Lessons 37 to 40: free cook. Player is given a pantry and no dish name, and is scored on whether they produce something the cuisine would recognize.

**Free tier** draws random dishes from every cuisine with cards attached, but no sequencing and no track progress. It is a sampler that shows the player what the track would teach them.

## Monetization

Free sampler, two paid features. No ads at all, in any tier: ads cut against the "AI for HI" positioning and against the calm, commute-length feel. Ad-free is not a selling point if there were never ads.

| Tier | What you get | Price (proposed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Random dishes across all cuisines, score, judge line, cards | $0 |
| Cook It Tonight | Turn any attempt into a real recipe with quantities, steps, and a shopping list; export to Reminders or Notes | $3.99/month or included in any track |
| Deeper Cuisine Path | One 40-lesson linear track with the chef's name on it | $9.99 per track, one-time |
| All Access | Every track plus Cook It Tonight | $4.99/month or $39.99/year |

One-time track purchases matter: a person who wants to learn Italian cooking and nothing else should be able to pay once and own it. Subscriptions are for the people who want everything.

Cook It Tonight is the bridge from game to kitchen and should be the first paid feature built, because it also proves the educational claim: people actually cook what the game taught them.

## Content pipeline

Claude drafts, a chef reviews, the chef's name goes on the track. Video comes after the track has proven people finish it.

1. **Profiles and cards drafted by Claude** from public cooking knowledge. Ingredient lists and ratios are facts, not copyrightable; prose is written fresh. Every profile cites two or three reference sources in an internal notes field.
2. **Chef review per cuisine**: one working cook with real standing in that cuisine, paid a flat fee of a few hundred dollars, red-pens the 40 lessons and the profiles. The chef catches what the internet repeats wrong, e.g. English-language Sichuan material over-indexing on heat and missing numbing, sour, and fragrance.
3. **Chef's name on the track**: "The Sichuan Track, reviewed by \[name\]." This is the marketing.
4. **Video series (funded phase)**: the named chef demonstrates each dish on camera; Moe produces and records. Only after completion-rate data from step 3 justifies the cost.

The chef relationship is a ladder: review fee, then name on the track, then a filmed series with revenue share. Each step is a small ask that builds on the last.

**Launch cuisines**: start with three. Italian (broadest appeal), Sichuan (sharpest grammar, best decoys), Japanese home cooking (clearest ratios, e.g. 1:1:1 soy, mirin, sake). Add Mexican and Thai next.

## Technical requirements

Native iOS, Swift, SwiftUI, iOS 17+. Content and scoring live on-device so the free game works offline on a subway. The LLM judge is the only network call and degrades gracefully to a canned line from the score breakdown when offline.

**Stack**

| Layer | Choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UI | SwiftUI, SpriteKit for the vessel scene only | SwiftUI for menus and cards; SpriteKit for drag, drop, and particle effects in the pan |
| Audio | AVAudioEngine | Low-latency layered playback: a sizzle loop plus one-shot adds |
| Scoring | Pure Swift module, no dependencies | Deterministic, unit-testable, runs offline |
| Content | Bundled JSON per cuisine, versioned; remote updates via a signed manifest | Ship new tracks without an App Store release |
| Judge | Anthropic API via a thin backend proxy, response cached by (dish id, score pattern) | Never ship the API key in the app; cache cuts cost by an order of magnitude |
| Persistence | SwiftData | Progress, purchases, card history |
| Purchases | StoreKit 2 | Tracks as non-consumables, All Access as auto-renewing subscription |
| Backend | Minimal: Cloudflare Workers or a small Swift Vapor service | Proxy, cache, content manifest; no accounts in v1 |

**Data model (core types)**

- `Cuisine`: id, name, signature envelope (heat, acid, umami, sweet, numbing ranges), ingredient families
- `Ingredient`: id, name, family, default unit, density (for unit-to-weight), icon, sound cue
- `DishProfile`: id, cuisine id, name, vessel, required families, optional families, forbidden ingredients, ratio bands, technique tags, card id
- `Attempt`: dish id, list of (ingredient id, amount, unit), timestamp
- `ScoreBreakdown`: coverage, ratio fit, signature, technique, total, misses
- `Card`: id, title, body, rule line, try-tonight line
- `Track`: cuisine id, ordered lesson ids, pass threshold per lesson, chef credit

**Testing**: the scoring module gets golden tests: a known-good attempt per dish must score 85+, a known-off-cuisine attempt must score under 40. Chef review feeds these fixtures.

**Privacy**: no account, no email, no analytics beyond Apple's. Attempts are sent to the judge proxy anonymously.

## MVP scope and build order

The MVP is one cuisine, ten dishes, the full scoring engine, sound, and cards. No tracks, no purchases, no backend. It exists to answer one question: do people finish a five-round session and come back tomorrow?

1. **Scoring module first**, with golden tests, before any UI. Ten Sichuan dish profiles hand-authored by Claude. This is the risky part; if the scoring feels arbitrary, nothing else matters.
2. **Vessel scene**: one wok, drag-and-drop ingredients, amount stepper, placeholder sounds. Ugly is fine.
3. **Judge line**: canned feedback from the score breakdown. No LLM yet.
4. **Cards**: ten cards, one per dish, shown on submit.
5. **Session loop**: five rounds, summary screen, local progress.
6. **TestFlight to 20 people**. Measure: session completion, day-two return, which dishes score lowest (those are either badly profiled or genuinely hard; find out which).
7. **Then** the LLM judge, then art and real sound, then StoreKit and the first paid track.

Out of scope for MVP, and decline if it creeps in: accounts, social features, leaderboards, multiplayer, recipe import, user-generated dishes, Android.
