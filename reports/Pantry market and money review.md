# Cuisine tracks, not subscriptions, carry Pantry

Pantry becomes profitable almost immediately in the narrow sense, because a solo, no-ads, no-accounts app has cash costs of about $99 a year plus pennies of judge-model spend; it becomes a meaningful income only if it finds tens of thousands of organic installs a month, and nothing in the evidence says that will happen by default. On the model below, built from sourced benchmarks where they exist and labelled assumptions where they do not, Pantry nets about **$0.27 per install in the base case** ($0.07 low, $1.07 high) after Apple's 15%, which means **about 3,700 installs a month for $1k, 37,000 for $10k and 187,000 for $50k** of monthly net revenue. The $9.99 cuisine tracks carry 48 to 60% of revenue in every case, the $3.99 subscription 38 to 42%, and grocery referral 3 to 10%; the track conversion rate, the single most important number, has no published benchmark and is an assumption to test. Paid acquisition does not pay back: US Apple Ads installs cost a median $2.91 to $4.63 against that $0.27. Demand for the habit (short daily thinking games) and for the subject (technique-first cooking, Chinese cooking in particular) is large and well evidenced, but paid demand for a cooking-knowledge game is unproven, and the closest comparable, Parsnip, shows a small audience after four years with far more resources. The findings argue for making the track the hero purchase, replacing the monthly-only subscription with a one-time unlock or an annual plan, treating grocery hand-off as a convenience and not a revenue line, and spending the first months measuring track conversion and organic reach.

A note on labels used throughout: figures with a link are sourced; "assumption" marks a number I chose; "computed" marks my arithmetic on sourced figures; "not found" means the research looked and found nothing citable.

## The closest lookalikes are small, and the big cooking games earn in ways Pantry forbids

The nearest product to Pantry's pitch is Parsnip, the "Duolingo for cooking": short game-like lessons with stars and streaks, backed by Duolingo's co-founders, live and updated through June 2026. It sells a subscription at App Store price points of **$6.99 to $7.00 a month and $24.00 to $49.99 a year**, and shows **419 US App Store ratings** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/parsnip-learn-to-cook/id1592325213)). A third-party profile credits it with "over 80,000 downloads across iOS and Android" and about 16 employees, both undated and unverified ([yespress.io](https://yespress.io/parsnip.md)). Its revenue, subscriber count and seed amount were not found. Read plainly, a funded team with the best possible backers has not found a large audience for gamified cooking lessons in four years. That is evidence the category is hard to grow, not evidence that the market is absent.

The mechanic itself has free neighbours. Forkdle, DishGuise and Phoodle are daily guess-the-dish web puzzles from individuals or tiny teams with no paid tier found; DishGuise, the closest to Pantry's loop (ingredients, then a recipe), drew one upvote on Product Hunt ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/dishguise-a-game-of-culinary-deduction)), and Phoodle's iOS app shows "1k+" downloads and 10 ratings ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/phoodle/6478786656)). The one precedent for scoring a dish the player builds is Chef League (2019), built by a Harvard engineering professor, which has 27 ratings and no update since February 2021 ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/chef-league-challenges-you-to-improvise-recipes-like-the-pros/); [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/chef-league/1437820584?country=us)). No active, commercially visible game that scores a composed dish against a cuisine's logic was found. That search was a handful of web queries, so the niche is "apparently unoccupied", which is consistent with both an opening and a graveyard.

The mainstream cooking-game genre proves the theme and nothing else. Cooking Fever has 345K US ratings and sells packs from $1.49 to $24.99 alongside advertising ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cooking-fever-restaurant-game/id714796093)); Good Pizza, Great Pizza reports more than 300 million downloads from a team of 12 with revenue about 60% ads and 40% in-app purchases ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/good-pizza-great-pizza-touts-in-game-events-for-10th-anniversary/)). These are volume businesses built on ads, timers and consumables, all of which Pantry's guardrails rule out, so their revenue does not transfer.

Recipe apps set the price expectations Pantry's subscription will be judged against. The table is from US App Store listings read on 8 October 2026 unless noted; rating counts indicate audience tier, not downloads.

Duolingo is the ceiling reference for gamified learning: in Q2 2026 it reported 140.6 million monthly users and 12.7 million paid subscribers, with subscriptions $258.0 million of $298.5 million revenue ([Duolingo shareholder letter, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)). ## Median apps keep 1% of players at day 30 and convert 1 to 2% to paid

The benchmarks describe a harsh middle. The median North American mobile game in 2025 retained **23.3% of players on day 1, 5.0% on day 7 and 1.2% on day 30**; the top 10% reached 36.9%, 12.6% and 4.8% ([GameAnalytics 2026](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-27-2026-mobile-pc-benchmarks_compressed.pdf)). Marketing-platform datasets show much higher figures (Adjust's iOS medians were 27%, 14% and 8%, in a 2024 article) because they measure apps that buy marketing ([Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/what-makes-a-good-retention-rate/)); for an unfunded solo launch the GameAnalytics distribution is the honest planning base. Genre-level, iOS-only and education-app retention figures were not found.

For subscriptions, RevenueCat's 2026 dataset of more than 115,000 apps gives a median download-to-paid conversion by day 35 of **2.0% overall, 1.4% for low-priced apps and 1.0% for gaming apps**, with top quartiles above 3.7% and 2.3% respectively; freemium apps convert 2.1% against 10.7% for hard paywalls ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Monthly plans leak: the median low-priced app keeps **10.8% of monthly subscribers at twelve months**, against 36% on annual plans, and realises **$6.67 per payer in month one and $10.69 after a year** ([RevenueCat insights](https://revenuecat.com/sosa-26-insights/)). Median revenue per install at day 60 is $0.11 for low-priced apps, $0.14 for gaming apps and $0.55 for North America across all categories ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). The same dataset is the best base rate for outcomes: a year after launch the middle 50% of subscription apps earn **$16 to $429 a month**, 17% of new apps reach $1k monthly recurring revenue within two years and 4.6% reach $10k (9% for gaming apps) ([RevenueCat insights](https://revenuecat.com/sosa-26-insights/)). Three caveats apply. These benchmarks describe apps whose main business is the subscription, whereas Pantry's is a utility add-on to a game. No benchmark for monthly plans under $5 was found, nor the renewal curve at one, three and six months. Whether RevenueCat's revenue figures are before or after store commission was not confirmed.

**For one-time content packs there is no benchmark at all.** No source reached gives a conversion rate for non-consumable unlocks or content packs in educational or premium-content games, nor a repeat-purchase rate across packs. The nearest adjacent figures are loosely sourced: "less than 5%" of mobile gamers ever pay, and 0.5 to 1% is called acceptable for casual genres ([Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/arpdau-benchmarks-by-game-genre)). Every track number in the model below is therefore an assumption.

Acquisition costs dwarf these yields. On Apple Ads search results in the US in 2025 the median cost per install was **$4.63 for games and $2.91 for education** apps ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks?format=md)); a weaker secondary source puts US iOS casual-game installs across channels at $0.90 to $2.50 ([SEM Nexus](https://semnexus.com/cpi-benchmarks-app-category-platform-2026/)). The share of installs an indie app gets organically was not found.

## Apple takes 15%, but only once the Small Business Program is in force

A solo US developer pays **$99 a year** for the Apple Developer Program ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/compare-memberships/)) and, once enrolled in the App Store Small Business Program, **15% commission** on in-app purchases and subscriptions. The programme covers developers with proceeds of no more than $1 million in the prior and current calendar year, new developers qualify, enrolment is not automatic, and the reduced rate starts 15 days after the end of the fiscal month in which enrolment is approved ([Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). Without it the standard rate is 30% ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3013090)). The second-year subscription discount to 15% gives a programme member nothing extra in the US ([Phiture](https://phiture.com/blog/apple-new-app-store-terms-2026/)); Apple's page also mentions a 10% rate whose scope is ambiguous and should not be assumed for US sales. Computed: a $9.99 track nets 9.99 × 0.85 = **$8.49**, and a $3.99 payment nets 3.99 × 0.85 = **$3.39**; at 30% they net $6.99 and $2.79. Enrolling before the first sale is worth 15 points of revenue on every early purchase.

Two further rules matter. In the US storefront apps may link to web checkout for digital goods, and Apple currently collects nothing on those purchases under the Epic v. Apple contempt order; the Supreme Court granted review on 30 June 2026 with a ruling expected by June 2027, and the Ninth Circuit has said Apple may be allowed a cost-based fee ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/supreme-court-apple-epic-contempt-app-store-commission); [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/101339/supreme-court-to-hear-apples-appeal-of-app-store-contempt-ruling)). My judgment is that web checkout is not worth pursuing for Pantry: restoring a web purchase inside an app with no accounts and no email is awkward, and the saving is 15 points on an unsettled legal footing. Grocery orders are different. Guideline 3.1.3(e) requires that physical goods consumed outside the app be paid for by means other than in-app purchase ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), so a link hand-off to a grocer carries no Apple commission. The final part of the guidelines page was not read; no rule against affiliate links for physical goods is known.

## Grocery referral pays about 90 cents an order, if the door is open

Instacart's Developer Platform is the only large programme built for this hand-off. An API call returns a link to an Instacart-hosted recipe or shopping-list page, the documented attribution carries partner and campaign ids and no end-user identifier, and Instacart reviews "your integrated app or website" before issuing a production key and an invitation to its affiliate programme on Impact ([IDP conversions and payments](https://docs.instacart.com/developer_platform_api/guide/concepts/launch_activities/conversions_and_payments); [IDP approval process](https://docs.instacart.com/developer_platform_api/guide/concepts/launch_activities/approval_process)). That fits Pantry's no-accounts rule as documented. The API key must sit server-side, so the judge proxy or a second small endpoint would mint links. The commission rate is not public. The only Instacart-owned figure is **3% of qualifying purchases** on its creator affiliate page ([Instacart](https://instacart.com/affiliate)); a directory lists "up to $10" per new customer with a 7-day cookie, unconfirmed ([Referly](https://marketplace.referly.so/affiliate-programs/instacart)); a partner's explainer reports payout about 55 days after the month of purchase ([Jupiter](https://www.jupiter.co/blog/instacart-affiliate-for-food-creators)). One Instacart page, read on 8 October 2026, says **new applications are currently closed with no waitlist** ([Instacart](https://company.instacart.com/business/developers)), although the developer docs were still being updated in June 2026. That conflict must be resolved directly with Instacart before anything is built.

The alternatives are thinner. Amazon pays **1% on Grocery and Amazon Fresh** plus a $1 bounty for a first Fresh purchase ([Amazon Associates](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ?lang=en)) and bars Associates links in mobile apps without a signed amendment ([Amazon Associates help](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GFP8AD2UDZDQTJPP)). Walmart publishes no grocery rate ([CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/walmart-creator-program/)). Kroger's only listed figure is 3.2% on ship-to-home orders, last updated January 2025 ([FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/kroger-affiliate-program)). Weee!, the best-stocked national source for a Sichuan pantry, is listed at a **flat $8 per new-customer order** with a 7-day cookie and network-reported earnings of $0.11 per click ([FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/sayweee-com-affiliate-program/)). The Mala Market shows no programme ([The Mala Market](https://themalamarket.com/pages/about-us-1)).

The funnel is the unknown. A recipe basket through a shoppable button was about $30 and ten items in Chicory's data from around 2021 ([Chicory](https://chicory.co/blog-feed/why-shoppability-is-a-worthwile-investment)). Computed: 3% of $30 is **$0.90 per order**, and 3% of a $100 full shop is $3.00. The share of recipe viewers who tap a shop button and the share of hand-offs that become orders were not found from any neutral source, and no recipe app's affiliate earnings were found in public. The middleware companies in this space describe themselves as funded by brands and retailers, which suggests, by inference from silence, that commission alone does not sustain them. Whether Instacart's catalogue matches doubanjiang or Chinkiang vinegar well is untested.

## The habit and the subject are both large; paid demand for the combination is unproven

Adults play short daily thinking games at enormous scale. NYT Games logged more than 11.2 billion puzzles solved in 2025, with Wordle at 4.2 billion ([NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/hardest-wordle-connections-games-2025/6436464/)); 212.3 million Americans play video games at least an hour a week, mobile is the most popular platform at 80%, and puzzle is the top mobile genre at 66% ([ESA Essential Facts 2026, via Branding in Asia](https://www.brandinginasia.com/67-of-americans-now-play-video-games-weekly-as-average-age-hits-37/)). The Times does not disclose Games subscribers or the paying share, and no other daily-games publisher does either, so this proves the habit and not the willingness to pay.

Interest in learning to cook by principle is also visible, mostly in free video. J. Kenji López-Alt's channel has over 1.7 million subscribers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Kenji_L%C3%B3pez-Alt)), Made With Lau 1.8 million ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_With_Lau)) and Chinese Cooking Demystified about 971,000 ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC54SLBnD5k5U3Q6N__UjbAw/)). NYT Cooking drew more than 456 million visits in 2024 behind a $50-a-year paywall ([Bloomberg via The Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2025/mar/18/new-york-times-cooking-app-finds-right-ingredients/)); its subscriber count is undisclosed. Ingredient access for the first cuisine is no longer a barrier: Weee! passed $1 billion in annualised revenue growing 30 to 40% a year ([South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/us/economy-trade-business/article/3344657/immigration-slows-americas-appetite-ethnic-groceries-keeps-growing)). On restaurant adoption, Japanese (48% of US operators) runs well ahead of regional Chinese (28%) ([Datassential](https://datassential.com/resource/asian-food-trends-2025-the-evolution-toward-culinary-authenticity/)), which mildly supports Japanese as a follow-on; nothing found sizes Italian, and no direct measure of US interest in cooking Sichuan food at home was found.

The surveys complicate the "get better at cooking" pitch. In Instacart's September 2025 survey 62% of US adults rated themselves very or extremely confident cooks, the top obstacles were time (20%) and exhaustion (19%), and only 7% named lack of skill ([Instacart](https://company.instacart.com/ideas/cooking-at-home-survey)). The felt skill gap sits with Gen Z, 80% of whom say they cook less than they would like in a grill brand's metro-only survey ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/05/14/3081187/0/en/gen-z-is-the-foodie-generation.html)). All these surveys are brand-commissioned, and none asked whether people want to improve. The implication is to sell Pantry as an enjoyable commute-length game that happens to teach, which sidesteps the time objection, and not as self-improvement. One randomised study supports the teaching claim: among 1,087 adults in Norway and the UK, an online cooking game with feedback after each dish changed self-reported food-handling behaviour a week later where a video alone only raised awareness ([Nofima](https://nofima.com/results/spill-online-og-unnga-matforgiftning/)). It concerns food safety, not skill, and no study of games improving adult cooking skill was found.

## Pantry nets about $0.27 per install in the base case

### The assumptions

The model counts first-year net revenue from 1,000 installs, after Apple's 15% and before income tax, the $99 fee and judge-model costs. Net prices are $8.49 per track and $3.39 per subscription payment, as computed above.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Installs buying any track | 0.5% | 1.5% | 4.0% | **Assumption to test; no benchmark exists.** Anchored loosely on 0.5 to 1% casual payer share and 1.0 to 2.1% subscription conversion |
| Tracks per buyer | 1.0 | 1.2 | 1.5 | Assumption. Only 1.0 is possible until a second cuisine ships |
| Installs starting the subscription | 0.5% | 1.0% | 2.5% | Low is my assumption (half the gaming median, since the plan is an add-on); base is RevenueCat's gaming median; high sits between the gaming top quartile (2.3%) and the low-priced top quartile (3.7%) |
| Payments per subscriber, year one | 1.6 | 3.0 | 5.3 | Low is computed from RevenueCat's low-priced payer value, 10.69 / 6.67 = 1.6; high is computed as a constant-churn curve ending at 10.8% in month twelve (monthly retention 0.108^(1/12) = 0.831; payments = (1 − 0.108) / (1 − 0.831) = 5.3); base is my assumption between them |
| Grocery orders per 1,000 installs | 2 | 15 | 60 | Assumption; no funnel benchmark exists. 0.2% of installs × 1 order; 1% × 1.5 orders; 3% × 2 orders |
| Commission per order | $0.90 | $0.90 | $1.80 | 3% of a $30 basket (sourced inputs); high assumes a $60 basket, 3% × 60 |

### Revenue per 1,000 installs

| Revenue line | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tracks | 5 buyers × 1.0 × $8.49 = **$42.45** | 15 × 1.2 × $8.49 = **$152.82** | 40 × 1.5 × $8.49 = **$509.40** |
| Subscription | 5 × 1.6 × $3.39 = **$27.12** | 10 × 3.0 × $3.39 = **$101.70** | 25 × 5.3 × $3.39 = **$449.18** |
| Grocery referral | 2 × $0.90 = **$1.80** | 15 × $0.90 = **$13.50** | 60 × $1.80 = **$108.00** |
| Total per 1,000 installs | **$71.37** | **$268.02** | **$1,066.58** |

| Share: tracks / subscription / grocery | 59% / 38% / 3% | 57% / 38% / 5% | 48% / 42% / 10% |

The subscription line can be checked against a sourced figure. The base case's $101.70 net is 101.70 / 0.85 = $119.65 gross per 1,000 installs, or $0.12 per install, which matches RevenueCat's day-60 medians of $0.11 for low-priced apps and $0.14 for gaming apps. The track and grocery lines cannot be checked against anything.

### Installs needed each month

In steady state, monthly net revenue is roughly monthly installs times first-year value per install; a new app takes up to a year to reach that state, though most of the value arrives in the first weeks. Required installs are the target divided by the per-install value (for example $1,000 / $0.26802 = 3,731).

| Monthly net target | Low ($0.07137) | Base ($0.26802) | High ($1.06658) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | 14,011 | 3,731 | 938 |
| $10,000 | 140,115 | 37,311 | 9,376 |
| $50,000 | 700,574 | 186,553 | 46,879 |

Comparables give these numbers scale. If Parsnip's unverified 80,000 downloads accrued over roughly 54 months, that is about 80,000 / 54 = 1,480 a month (computed, on an undated figure); at the base value that pace yields 1,480 × $0.268 = about $397 a month, inside RevenueCat's $16 to $429 middle band. So the base case at Parsnip's pace is a few hundred dollars a month, $1k needs two and a half times that pace, and $10k needs twenty-five times it. The $50k target in the base case requires about 187,000 installs a month, which is in the region of what Sensor Tower showed for Cooking Fever on US iOS in early 2025 (about 44K per period, apparently weekly, though the chart's time unit is not explicit) ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q1-ios-top-5-cooking%20games-units-us-6012d15b241bc16eb82bc0b7)). That target is out of reach without either high-case conversion or a breakout.

### Which line carries it, and whether ads can pay back

Tracks carry the model in every case, and they rest entirely on an untested conversion rate and on shipping more cuisines: with Sichuan alone the base track line is 15 × 1.0 × $8.49 = $127.35, a sixth lower. The subscription is a solid second line on paper but the weakest in logic, for reasons the next section covers. Grocery referral is immaterial below the high case: even 60 orders per 1,000 installs produces a tenth of revenue.

Paid acquisition does not pay back. Against the sourced Apple Ads medians, the base case returns 0.268 / 2.91 = 9 cents on the dollar for education-priced installs and 0.268 / 4.63 = 6 cents for games-priced ones; the high case returns 37 and 23 cents. Only the high case against the cheapest end of a weakly sourced casual range (1.067 / 0.90 = 119%) clears break-even, and that needs top-quartile monetisation and bottom-quartile install cost at once. Growth has to be organic and owned.

One cost deserves a decision now. If each round calls a language model for the judge line, an illustrative 300 tokens in and 300 out costs about $0.00018 on a small model and $0.0036 on a mid-tier one at October 2026 list prices ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). Assuming 30 rounds per install over its life (my assumption), 1,000 installs cost 30,000 × $0.00018 = $5.40 on the small model, 2% of base revenue, or 30,000 × $0.0036 = $108 on the mid-tier one, 40% of it. Free-tier judge lines should run on a small model or stay canned. Proxy hosting cost was not researched.

## A $3.99 monthly plan for $4.99 features is the weakest part of the design

The subscription sells what Paprika sells once for $4.99 and Mela once for $6.99, at a monthly price close to NYT Cooking's, which comes with about 24,000 recipes. A player who compares will see the mismatch, and the benchmarks say monthly plans at low prices keep one subscriber in ten for a year. The arithmetic shows how little the monthly plan earns for that friction. A base-case subscriber nets 3.0 × $3.39 = $10.17 in year one, and a low-case one 1.6 × $3.39 = $5.42. A one-time "Cook It Tonight" unlock at $9.99 nets $8.49, which beats the low case outright and matches the base case if it attracts 10.17 / 8.49 = 1.2 times as many buyers, a 20% lift that a no-subscription offer in this category plausibly earns (assumption to test). An annual plan at $19.99 nets 19.99 × 0.85 = $16.99 up front, close to the high-case monthly subscriber's 5.3 × $3.39 = $17.97, and RevenueCat's low-priced annual plans retain 36% into year two.

My recommendation is to stop treating the subscription as a pillar. The strongest version of the model makes the cuisine track the purchase the whole app points at, includes the cooking utilities for the cuisines a player owns or sells them as a single one-time unlock in the $6.99 to $9.99 range, and offers an annual option only if there is a recurring reason to pay, such as new dishes arriving every month. A subscription that unlocks every current and future cuisine is a more defensible recurring offer than one that unlocks a shopping list. A three-cuisine bundle (for example $24.99, netting $21.24; the price is an assumption) becomes available once Italian and Japanese exist and is the cheapest way to raise tracks per buyer. The steelman for keeping $3.99 a month is that recurring revenue compounds and that subscriptions are 86% of Duolingo's revenue; the reply is that Duolingo's subscription removes friction from the core loop, while Pantry's sells an accessory with cheap one-time substitutes.

Grocery hand-off should ship, if at all, because "shop these ingredients" finishes the promise of cooking it tonight, and should be budgeted at zero. Paid acquisition should be off the table until measured value per install exceeds $1.

## Conclusion: five things to settle first

The research changes the question. Profitability in the accounting sense is nearly automatic for a solo app with no ad spend and no servers beyond a proxy; the open question is whether Pantry can reach the low tens of thousands of monthly installs that turn $0.27 into an income, and that depends on distribution the benchmarks cannot predict. The honest prior, from RevenueCat's base rates and Parsnip's example, is a few hundred dollars a month a year after launch, with roughly a one-in-six chance of passing $1k within two years. What would move Pantry out of that band is a track conversion rate well above 1.5% combined with an owned channel into the audience that already watches technique-first Chinese cooking video.

Five things are worth testing or deciding first, in order. First, measure track conversion, since it drives more than half of revenue and has no benchmark: App Store Connect reports installs and in-app purchase units without any added analytics, and the first 2,000 to 3,000 installs will show whether the rate is nearer 0.5% or 4%. Second, decide the shape of "Cook It Tonight" before launch, between monthly subscription, one-time unlock and annual plan; the evidence here favours a one-time unlock or bundling it into tracks, and changing a subscription after launch is harder than adding one. Third, test organic reach early and cheaply, by putting the existing browser stand-in or a short clip in front of the Chinese-cooking video and forum audience and counting who asks for the app, because installs and not conversion are the binding constraint at every target. Fourth, read day-1, day-7 and day-30 retention from Apple's analytics against the 23% / 5% / 1.2% medians and the 37% / 13% / 5% top decile, since a game that retains at the median cannot earn the track conversion the model needs. Fifth, clear three small administrative items that are cheap now and costly later: enrol in the Small Business Program before the first sale, ask Instacart directly whether Developer Platform applications are open before designing anything around it, and fix the judge line to a small model or canned text for free players.
