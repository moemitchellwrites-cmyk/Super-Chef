# Demand signals for a cooking-knowledge game on iOS (as of 8 October 2026)

Reading notes for the writer:
- US first; global figures are marked "global".
- Figures older than two years (before October 2024) are marked **[OLD]**.
- "Not found" means I searched and did not find a citable figure; nothing here is interpolated. Arithmetic I did myself is in Inferences, never in Cited Findings.
- Several figures came from secondary coverage of a primary source (named in each line). Where I could only see a company claim relayed by a low-authority outlet, that is said.

## 1. Casual knowledge and daily-puzzle games as a proxy (NYT Games, LinkedIn, Puzzmo, Apple News+); share that pays

### Takeaway
The daily-puzzle habit is very large (NYT Games: more than 11.2 billion puzzles solved in 2025, Wordle about 11.5 million plays a day), but the Times does not disclose Games subscribers or paying share, and no other daily-games publisher discloses one either. The proxy shows that adults will do a short daily thinking game; it does not show that they will pay for one, or that they want a cooking one.

### Cited Findings
- NYT Games, 2025 (global, company figure): more than 11.2 billion puzzles solved; Wordle 4.2 billion; Connections 1.6 billion solves; Strands 1.5 billion; Mini Crossword 1.4 billion. Reported 29 Dec 2025. — [NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/hardest-wordle-connections-games-2025/6436464/)
- NYT Games, 2024 (global, NYT spokesperson via The Verge, June 2025): 11.1 billion total plays; Wordle 5.3 billion; Connections 3.3 billion "completed games"; Strands 1.3 billion. — [Wikipedia: The New York Times Games, citing The Verge 10 Jun 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Games)
- Wordle plays by year: 4.8 billion (2023), 5.3 billion (2024), 4.2 billion (2025, about 11.5 million a day); the publisher says the numbers swing year to year depending on virality. Article dated 3 Aug 2026; context is a Wordle TV show meant to "re-energize" lapsed players. — [A Media Operator](https://www.amediaoperator.com/analysis/wordle-tv-show-new-york-times-games-jonathan-knight/)
- NYT Games had 11 games after Crossplay launched (20 Jan 2026); Crossplay passed 1 million mobile downloads by 6 Feb 2026. For comparison the same article gives 2025 download estimates of 3.5 million for Words With Friends and 8.1 million installs for Scrabble Go. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/nyt-games-multiplayer-word-game-crossplay-surpasses-one-million-downloads)
- **[OLD]** "Over 10 million daily players" across NYT Games platforms (Adweek, Feb 2024); "over one million" Games premium subscribers (Digiday, Mar 2023); Games app average daily active users rose from 886,000 to over 2.6 million between Oct 2022 and 2023 (Vanity Fair, Dec 2023). — [Wikipedia: The New York Times Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Games)
- NYT Company Q2 2026 (quarter ended 30 Jun 2026): 13.35 million total subscribers, 12.80 million digital-only, about 280,000 digital net adds in the quarter, digital-only ARPU $9.94. No separate Games or Cooking subscriber counts are given. — [NYT Co. 8-K press release, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000071691/000007169126000032/pressrelease6302026.htm)
- A September 2026 trade piece confirms the Times has not broken out Games subscriber figures. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/new-york-times-gives-games-subscribers-5-to-6-new-puzzles-a-week/)
- About half of Times subscribers pay for the all-product bundle; the rest buy individual products; Games, Cooking and Wirecutter are credited with driving digital growth; news-only subscriptions declined in 2025 (AP, Jan 2026). — [WTOP / AP](https://wtop.com/consumer-news/2026/01/this-just-in-from-the-new-york-times-its-first-2-player-game-and-an-evolving-business-model/)
- LinkedIn games (Aug 2025): no player count disclosed beyond "millions of professionals" daily; 86% of players return the next day and 82% are still playing a week later (company figures); six games; LinkedIn says Gen Z plays most. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-adds-another-puzzle-game-mini-sudoku/757492/)
- Puzzmo (Hearst; article Mar 2025): launch-key campaign drew 104,000 players with no ad spend; Zach Gage's apps had 15 million installs at acquisition; no subscriber count published. — [INMA](https://www.inma.org/blogs/ideas/post.cfm/hearst-cracks-the-engagement-code-with-puzzmo)

### Inferences
- The only hard paying-share data point for NYT Games is old (over 1 million Games subscribers in Mar 2023, against "over 10 million daily players" in Feb 2024). Those two figures are from different dates and different bases, so they should not be divided into a conversion rate; at most they suggest paying users were a small minority of players.
- Total NYT Games plays were flat from 2024 to 2025 (11.1 to 11.2 billion) while Wordle fell 21% (5.3 to 4.2 billion): the habit is holding but spreading over more games, and single-title virality decays.
- The Connections figures (3.3 billion in 2024, 1.6 billion in 2025) are labelled differently in the two sources ("completed games" vs "solves") and may not be the same metric. Treat the apparent halving as unverified.
- This audience is a proxy for "adults who do a short daily brain game", not for cooking interest. Pantry sessions are also longer and more effortful than a Wordle.

### Gaps
- Current NYT Games subscriber count, weekly or monthly users, and paying share: not found (not disclosed).
- LinkedIn games absolute player numbers: not found (not disclosed).
- Puzzmo subscribers and active players: not found.
- Apple News+ puzzles audience: not found. Apple does not publish it.

## 2. Cooking and recipe app market

### Takeaway
Report-firm estimates put the US recipe-app market in the low hundreds of millions of dollars a year (one firm: about $239 million in 2024), growing about 12 to 13% a year, but these are low-reliability. The strongest hard signal is NYT Cooking: more than 456 million visits in 2024 behind a $50-a-year paywall, though its subscriber count is undisclosed.

### Cited Findings
- Market.us: US recipe app market $239.3 million in 2024, projected $269.2 million in 2025 and about $777 million by 2034 (12.5% CAGR); North America $255.4 million in 2024. The page is internally inconsistent (says "billion" once; lists a March 2024 publication date for 2024 base-year data; contradictory US share statements). — [Market.us](https://market.us/report/north-america-recipe-app-market/)
- Technavio (via Research and Markets, listing dated Aug 2026): global recipe apps market forecast to grow by $516.9 million over 2025 to 2030 at 12.3% CAGR; no absolute market size on the listing page. — [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/recipe-app)
- NYT Cooking (Bloomberg, 18 Mar 2025): more than 456 million visits to site and app in 2024; about 24,000 recipes; $6 a month or $50 a year standalone; YouTube views up 72% in 2024 with more than 4.3 million hours watched; most popular 2024 recipe (Marcella Hazan's bolognese) had more than 3.9 million views. No Cooking subscriber count given. Same article: Food Network viewership fell 26% from 2023 to 2024 (Nielsen) and cookbook sales have declined since 2022 (Circana BookScan, no figures). — [Bloomberg via The Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2025/mar/18/new-york-times-cooking-app-finds-right-ingredients/)
- **[OLD]** NYT Cooking had 120,000 subscriptions about a year and a half after its 2017 paywall (CNN, Nov 2018). This is the last standalone count I could confirm from a search result title; I did not open the article. — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2018/11/21/media/nyt-cooking)

### Inferences
- The two report-firm figures cannot be reconciled into a range for the same thing: one is a US level, the other a global increment. The honest statement is "low hundreds of millions of dollars in the US, low-double-digit growth, per report-selling firms whose pages contain errors".
- The decline in Food Network viewing and cookbook sales alongside growth in NYT Cooking and YouTube suggests cooking instruction is moving to apps and video rather than shrinking, but the Bloomberg piece gives no cookbook figures to size that.

### Gaps
- NYT Cooking current subscriber count: not found (the Times stopped breaking it out; widely repeated "over 1 million" figures were not confirmed in a source I opened).
- Number of US adults using recipe apps or sites: no current figure found. The only audited-style number surfaced was Nielsen's 86 million Americans visiting food and cooking websites, from 2014, which I did not open and is far too old to use.
- Allrecipes audience: People Inc.'s brand page returned an error; a third-party SEO blog headline claims about 52 million monthly Google visitors (undated, unverified). Treat as not found.
- Tasty audience: not found.

## 3. Home cooking interest

### Takeaway
Americans cook a lot and mostly say they are confident; lack of skill is rarely named as the barrier, except among Gen Z. Home cooking spiked in 2020 and fell back to pre-pandemic levels by 2022; I found no solid national figure for "wants to improve their cooking".

### Cited Findings
- **[OLD]** Gallup-Cookpad (published Oct 2023): US home-cooked meals fell to 8.2 per week in 2022, described as a historic low, from 9.4 in 2020; North America averaged 8.4 in 2022, the same as 2019 and the lowest of any region; women cooked 1.7 more meals a week than men. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/10/30/home-cooking-eating-pre-covid)
- Instacart / SurveyMonkey (11 Sep 2025, n=1,011 US adults, unweighted): 62% are very or extremely confident in their cooking; 14% unconfident or struggling; more than half of Gen Z are only moderately confident or less; one third cook five or more meals from scratch a week; top obstacles are time (20%) and exhaustion (19%), and only 7% cite lack of skill; saving money is the top motivation (35%); YouTube is a source of cooking inspiration for 36% and the top source for ages 18 to 44; 35% are self-taught; cooking classes taken by 10% of men and 5% of women. — [Instacart](https://company.instacart.com/ideas/cooking-at-home-survey)
- HelloFresh / Wakefield Research (10 to 22 Jun 2025, n=5,000 nationally representative US adults): 93% expect to cook as much or more in the next 12 months; 25% skip preparing certain foods because they are not confident with a knife; 56% get ideas from cookbooks, shows or websites, 55% from friends and family, 52% from social media; 86% repeat the same meals at least some of the time. Did not ask about wanting to improve. — [HelloFresh State of Home Cooking](https://www.hellofresh.com/eat/reports/stateofhomecooking)
- Current Backyard / Pollfish (released 14 May 2025, more than 6,500 respondents in the 25 largest US metros; a grill brand's survey): 80% of Gen Z say they cook less often than they would like, against 40% of other generations; Gen Z were more than twice as likely as older respondents to say cooking knowledge holds them back; Gen Z reports about 14.3 spicy-food meals a month against 11.6 for millennials and 7.2 for Gen X. — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/05/14/3081187/0/en/gen-z-is-the-foodie-generation.html)
- Flashfood / Harris Poll (Jan 2025): 81% of Americans say saving money on food is a priority in 2025. — [The National Provisioner](https://provisioneronline.com/articles/print/118169-frugal-americans-cook-more-at-home-in-2025)

### Inferences
- The skill gap as a felt problem is concentrated in Gen Z; older adults mostly rate themselves confident and name time and fatigue instead. A product pitched as "get better at cooking" meets a self-assessed need in a minority; one pitched as enjoyable and commute-length avoids the time objection.
- All of the 2025 surveys are brand-commissioned and each has an interest in a pro-cooking finding. The Current Backyard survey is metro-only and from an outdoor-cooking brand; weight it lightly.

### Gaps
- Share of US adults who say they want to improve their cooking: not found in any survey I opened.
- Pew, Gallup (post-2022), IFIC Food and Health Survey, Hunter Food Study 2025/2026 and Kroger/84.51° figures on home cooking: not found in the searches run. The Hunter results that surfaced were from 2020.
- Gallup-Cookpad data after 2022: not found.

## 4. Cooking education demand (classes, YouTube, teaching cookbooks)

### Takeaway
The clearest evidence of demand for technique-first cooking teaching is free video: explanatory channels hold 1 to 2 million subscribers each, including two devoted to Chinese home cooking. Paid class enrolments and cookbook unit sales are almost entirely undisclosed.

### Cited Findings
- J. Kenji López-Alt's YouTube channel: over 1.7 million subscribers and over 290 million views as of Aug 2026 (global). The Wok (2022) debuted at number one on the NYT Advice, How-To & Miscellaneous list and won a 2023 James Beard award; The Food Lab (2015) was an NYT bestseller and won a 2016 James Beard award. No unit sales given. — [Wikipedia: J. Kenji López-Alt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Kenji_L%C3%B3pez-Alt)
- Made With Lau (Cantonese home cooking): 1.8 million subscribers and 193 million views (infobox, undated; global); passed 1 million in Dec 2022; about 40% of viewers were in the US and Canada per the founder (CNN, May 2022, **[OLD]**). — [Wikipedia: Made With Lau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_With_Lau)
- Chinese Cooking Demystified: about 971,000 subscribers and 97.8 million views as of 15 May 2025 (global). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC54SLBnD5k5U3Q6N__UjbAw/)
- Samin Nosrat's Good Things (Random House, 2025): fourth on the Washington Post non-fiction hardcover list in Dec 2025 and ninth on the Los Angeles Times non-fiction list in Jan 2026. No unit sales. — [Wikipedia: Good Things (book)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Things_(book))
- NYT Cooking's most-watched YouTube video, a Claire Saffitz croissant tutorial, has more than 5.1 million views (Mar 2025). — [Bloomberg via The Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2025/mar/18/new-york-times-cooking-app-finds-right-ingredients/)
- MasterClass: no subscriber count is published; a statistics aggregator could cite only "over 1 million" Google Play downloads and 180+ courses in 11 categories, of which Food is one. — [Skillademia](https://www.skillademia.com/statistics/masterclass-statistics/)

### Inferences
- A channel that explains Chinese technique to English speakers can gather roughly 1 to 2 million subscribers worldwide over several years. With around 40% of Made With Lau's audience in the US and Canada (2022), the engaged North American audience per channel is likely in the hundreds of thousands, not millions. Subscribers are free and cumulative, so this is an upper bound on interest and says nothing about willingness to pay.

### Gaps
- Unit sales for Salt Fat Acid Heat, The Food Lab, The Wok, The Food of Sichuan and Good Things: not found. Two targeted searches returned only retailer listings; no Publishers Weekly or BookScan citation surfaced.
- Joshua Weissman subscriber count: not found in a source I opened.
- America's Test Kitchen online cooking school, Rouxbe, Sur La Table online enrolments; MasterClass subscribers and food-category enrolment: not found.

## 5. Interest in Chinese and Sichuan cooking in the US; Italian and Japanese as next cuisines

### Takeaway
Supply-side signals are positive and recent: Weee! passed $1 billion in annualised revenue growing 30 to 40% a year, and chili crisp has become a mainstream grocery item. But I found no direct US measure of interest in cooking Sichuan food at home, and restaurant-operator data shows regional Chinese (28% of operators) well behind Japanese (48%).

### Cited Findings
- Weee! (Asian and Latino online grocer; SCMP, 26 Feb 2026): annual revenue grew about 30 to 40% from 2023 to 2025; annualised revenue recently passed $1 billion; about a year from profitability; valued at $4.1 billion in 2022. — [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/us/economy-trade-business/article/3344657/immigration-slows-americas-appetite-ethnic-groceries-keeps-growing)
- Weee! (Sacra estimates): revenue about $810 million in 2023 and about $1.01 billion in 2024; 1.5 million people purchased in 2025; average spend about $250 a month; more than 50 million orders fulfilled by Aug 2025. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/weee)
- Chili crisp market (Growth Market Reports, page updated 27 Sep 2026; global, report-firm estimate): $2.06 billion in 2025 at manufacturer prices, forecast $3.58 billion by 2034 (6.3% CAGR); North America 17.5% of revenue in 2025. — [Growth Market Reports](https://growthmarketreports.com/report/chili-crisp-market)
- **[OLD]** Momofuku Goods had about $50 million in sales in 2023 and raised a $17.5 million Series A; Fly By Jing raised a $12 million Series B in 2023; MìLà raised $22.5 million (Forbes, Apr 2024). — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/andywang/2024/04/06/the-market-opportunity-for-chili-crunch-is-huge-so-the-battle-heats-up/)
- Fly By Jing shifted from direct-to-consumer to a retail focus in 2025, with product in Costco (citing Fast Company). No revenue figure. — [Wikipedia: Fly By Jing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly_By_Jing)
- Datassential (page last modified Sep 2025; "share of US operators" adopting each cuisine): Thai 53%, Japanese 48%, Korean 44%, Indian 37%, authentic/regional Chinese 28%, Vietnamese 23%. Sichuan flavours described as "gaining fast consumer acceptance" with no figure; mapo tofu over-indexes with millennials (appeal index 152, undefined on the page); soup dumplings up 47% on menus over four years. — [Datassential](https://datassential.com/resource/asian-food-trends-2025-the-evolution-toward-culinary-authenticity/)

### Inferences
- Applying the report firm's 17.5% North America share to its $2.06 billion global figure gives roughly $360 million for North American chili crisp in 2025. That is my arithmetic on a single low-reliability estimate, at manufacturer prices; use as an order of magnitude only.
- Asian-grocery growth partly reflects a growing Asian American population shopping for staples, not only non-Asian cooks learning the cuisine. It shows ingredient access is no longer a barrier; it does not show how many people want to learn Sichuan cooking.
- Japanese has wider restaurant adoption than regional Chinese on Datassential's measure, which mildly supports Japanese as a next cuisine. Nothing found speaks to Italian, which is so mainstream that trend sources do not track it as a trend (Marcella Hazan's bolognese being NYT Cooking's top 2024 recipe is one small indicator, cited in section 2).

### Gaps
- Google Trends direction for "Sichuan", "mapo tofu", "chili crisp", "doubanjiang": not found in reputable coverage.
- Retail scanner data (Circana, NielsenIQ, SPINS) on US chili crisp sales growth: not found.
- Lao Gan Ma US sales, Fly By Jing revenue, current Momofuku Goods sales: not found.
- The Mala Market sales or customers: not found. H Mart sales or store growth: not found.
- Count or growth of Sichuan restaurants in the US: not found.
- Relative interest in Italian vs Japanese vs Chinese home cooking (survey or search data): not found.

## 6. Mobile game audience overlap

### Takeaway
Most American adults play games weekly, mobile is the dominant platform, and puzzle is the top mobile genre, so the "plays thoughtful games on a phone" pool is not the constraint. Existing cooking-themed mobile games are large by downloads but are time-management or merge games that teach no cooking, so their audience is a weak proxy.

### Cited Findings
- ESA Essential Facts 2026 (published 6 Jun 2026, with YouGov; US): 212.3 million Americans aged 5 to 90 play at least an hour a week (67%), up 7.2 million on 2025; average player age 37; 53% of men and 46% of women play; weekly play by generation: millennials 71%, Gen X 56%, boomers 50%; among boomers more women (52%) than men (47%) play; mobile is the most popular platform at 80%; puzzle is the top mobile genre at 66%. — [Branding in Asia, summarising ESA](https://www.brandinginasia.com/67-of-americans-now-play-video-games-weekly-as-average-age-hits-37/)
- **[OLD]** Cooking Fever (Nordcurrent, time-management): 450 million downloads as of 2024 (global, lifetime; PocketGamer.biz, Jul 2024). — [Wikipedia: Cooking Fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_Fever)
- **[OLD]** Sensor Tower, Q4 2023, US "cooking games" (its category includes merge and bingo titles): Cooking Madness had more than 405,000 weekly active users and roughly $280,000 to $290,000 in revenue per period shown; Cooking Diary about 135,000 weekly active users; Gossip Harbor about 331,000. — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2023-q4-unified-top-5-cooking%20games-revenue-us-6012d15b241bc16eb82bc0b7)

### Inferences
- In late 2023 the leading cooking-themed games each had US weekly actives in the low hundreds of thousands. That is the scale of established, heavily marketed, free-to-play titles, and it is a ceiling reference rather than a target for a paid or premium knowledge game from a solo developer.
- These games monetise through in-app purchases and ads under time pressure, which Pantry's guardrails rule out. Their revenue does not transfer.

### Gaps
- Share of US mobile gamers who are women aged 25 to 54: not found. ESA gives gender and generation separately, and I saw only a secondary summary of the 2026 report, not the report itself.
- Current (2025 to 2026) US audience and revenue for cooking-themed mobile games: not found.
- Audience for any cooking game that teaches real cooking: not found; I did not find a comparable product with published numbers.

## 7. Willingness to pay for hobby-skill education apps (analogues)

### Takeaway
Duolingo is the one analogue with audited numbers: 12.7 million paid subscribers against 140.6 million monthly users in Q2 2026. Chess.com is large but does not disclose payers. Both are free-first products where a single-digit share of users pays.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo Q2 2026 (global; quarter ended 30 Jun 2026): 58.7 million DAUs (up 23% year on year), 140.6 million MAUs (up 10%), 12.7 million paid subscribers at period end (up 17%), revenue $298.5 million (up 18%), subscription revenue $258.0 million (up 22%). — [Duolingo 8-K shareholder letter, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)
- Chess.com (global; CEO Erik Allebest on the No Priors podcast, relayed by a startup news site, Aug 2026; not independently verified): over 250 million registered members, 10 million daily active users, more than $200 million annual revenue; paying subscribers not disclosed. — [StartupHub.ai](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/startup-news/2026/how-chess-com-bootstrapped-to-200m-revenue-and-250m-users)
- **[OLD]** Sherwood News (Jun 2024) estimated 1 to 1.5% of Chess.com's then roughly 150 million members paid, noting freemium businesses often reach 2 to 5%. This is the authors' estimate, not a company figure. — [Sherwood News](https://www.sherwood.news/culture/how-the-chess-com-empire-makes-more-than-usd100m-a-year)

### Inferences
- Duolingo's paid subscribers are 9.0% of MAUs and 21.6% of DAUs (12.7 / 140.6 and 12.7 / 58.7; my arithmetic on the filed figures). This is the best case in the category, from a company with a decade of optimisation and a family plan that counts each member.
- These analogues are habit products with a clear external goal (speak a language, raise a rating). Cooking has a real-world payoff too, but no analogue with published numbers exists for it.
- Combined with section 1, a planning assumption of low-single-digit conversion from free to paid is consistent with the evidence, and anything higher would need Pantry's own data.

### Gaps
- Yousician and Simply Piano (JoyTunes) paid subscriber counts: not found.
- Chess.com paid subscriber count: not disclosed.
- Duolingo's chess and music course usage: not in the Q2 2026 letter.

## 8. Evidence that games improve real cooking skill or food knowledge; edutainment retention for adult hobby learning

### Takeaway
One randomised study found an online cooking game changed adults' real food-handling behaviour where a video alone only raised awareness. I found nothing on games improving cooking skill itself, and nothing on retention of adult hobby-learning apps beyond company-reported figures.

### Cited Findings
- **[OLD]** Koch (Aarhus University) and Veflen (Nofima), "Fostering safe food handling among consumers: Causal evidence on game- and video-based online interventions" (reported May 2022): 1,087 participants aged 20 to 50 in Norway and the UK, plus 886 outside that age range, randomised to control, a two-minute WHO food-safety video, or the video plus an online cooking game with feedback after each of four dishes; follow-up at seven days. Both video and game groups became more aware of risks; only the game group changed food-preparation behaviour; effects were fairly consistent across ages. Not US; outcome is self-reported food-safety behaviour, not cooking skill. — [Nofima](https://nofima.com/results/spill-online-og-unnga-matforgiftning/)
- LinkedIn reports 86% next-day and 82% one-week return rates for its daily games (company figure, Aug 2025), the only published retention numbers for adult daily games found. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-adds-another-puzzle-game-mini-sudoku/757492/)

### Inferences
- The Koch and Veflen design (make a dish in a game, get feedback after each dish) is structurally close to Pantry's round-then-lesson-card loop, and it is the best available support for the claim that feedback inside a cooking game transfers to the kitchen. It supports "can change behaviour over a week", not "builds lasting skill".
- LinkedIn's retention figures come from games embedded in an app people already open daily, so they overstate what a standalone app should expect.

### Gaps
- Studies of games improving adult cooking skill, flavour knowledge or cuisine knowledge: not found. Search results were otherwise nutrition-literacy and children's serious-game work, which I did not open and which does not match Pantry's audience.
- Independent retention data for adult hobby-learning apps: not found.
